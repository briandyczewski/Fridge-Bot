Home/How it works

One photo per door close. Zero typing.

Fridge Bot turns a single still photo into a list of what's in your fridge and how long each thing has left. Here's every step, from the door shutting to the reminder on your phone.

  1. Door closes

    A light sensor notices the fridge has gone dark.

  2. One photo

    Its own small light comes on and it takes a single still.

  3. AI finds items

    A vision model outlines and names each thing on each shelf.

  4. Days left

    It compares when each item first appeared to typical shelf life.

  5. Your phone

    The list updates, and you get a nudge if something needs using.

STEP 01 · CAPTURE

It waits for the door to shut.

The camera sits on the top shelf, facing the rest of the fridge. A light sensor tells it when the door has closed, so it never fires while someone is standing there.

Then it switches on its own small light, takes one still photo through an anti-fog lens, and sends it over Wi-Fi. No live video, ever.

STEP 02 · RECOGNIZE

AI reads every shelf.

A vision model looks at the photo and draws a box around each item it can see: the milk, the eggs, the leftover chili, the four tomatoes in the crisper. It names each one and counts multiples.

For packaged food it can also read printed dates when the label faces the camera.

STEP 03 · TRACK

It remembers what it can't see.

Every item gets a "first seen" date the moment it appears. That's how Fridge Bot knows the chili has been in there three days, even if nobody wrote it down.

When something gets pushed behind the new groceries, it stays on your list from earlier photos, marked "last seen" with a date, until it's actually gone.

STEP 04 · REMIND

You hear about it in time to cook it.

Your list is sorted by what to eat first. When something is about to turn, you get one short nudge, early enough to plan dinner around it, often with a recipe idea that uses it up.

If the app guesses wrong, correct it with a tap. It learns from that.

Shelf-life logic

How "days left" gets estimated.

Each item starts from a typical fridge life for that kind of food, counted from the day it first showed up. Printed dates and your corrections override the estimate.
